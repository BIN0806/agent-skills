# Teach a topic

- Establish what the learner wants to do and their current understanding. Use a short diagnostic or existing learning record to choose one achievable next skill. Ground explanations and examples in trusted resources relevant to that goal.
- Teach the minimum knowledge needed for a concrete exercise. Let the learner attempt it, give specific feedback, and check that they can explain or reproduce the result. Distinguish recognition during the explanation from independent recall later.
- Revisit earlier material with spaced retrieval and interleave related skills as practice develops. Adjust difficulty from demonstrated performance. For quizzes, avoid answer-position, wording-length or formatting clues.
- Use the learner's preferred format. A visual or interactive lesson is helpful when it improves understanding. For a requested multi-session course, reuse the workspace's lessons, references, shared assets and progress conventions, and record results and the next practice target.

Create learning files when they serve the requested course or workspace. Preserve practice edits and record uncertainty about mastery until an independent task demonstrates it. Keep a one-off explanation focused on the question asked.
